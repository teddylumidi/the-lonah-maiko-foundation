Every girl deserves to grow up whole, safe and celebrated

In parts of Nyamira County, the December school holiday is when girls face the greatest pressure to undergo Female Genital Mutilation (FGM) and other harmful practices. Awareness alone is not always enough — girls also need safe spaces, trusted mentors and a positive way to mark growing up.

This December, the Lonah Maiko Foundation will host 300 girls aged 10–18 for a seven-day residential programme at Obwororo Academy, Ikonge. For one week they will learn about their bodies and their rights, gain practical life skills, build friendships with other girls and female mentors — and graduate with confidence, a certificate and an empowerment gift pack.

KSh 6,366 covers one girl's full place: accommodation, three meals a day, supervision, training, transport support and her personal gift pack.

We are asking partners, friends, churches, businesses and the Kenyan diaspora to help us raise KSh 1,909,740 before the camp opens on 16 December. Sponsor one girl, a group of girls, or give any amount you can.

Seven days that change a girl's future

  1. DAY1

    Self-discovery and confidence

    Identity, self-esteem, talents, dreams and aspirations.

  2. DAY2

    FGM awareness and prevention

    Understanding FGM, myths, health consequences, rights and positive alternatives.

  3. DAY3

    Puberty and menstrual health

    Body changes, menstruation, menstrual hygiene and personal care.

  4. DAY4

    Child protection and safety

    Children's rights, consent, boundaries, abuse prevention and reporting.

  5. DAY5

    Reproductive health and relationships

    Age-appropriate education on teenage pregnancy, early marriage and peer pressure.

  6. DAY6

    Leadership and life skills

    Communication, decision-making, financial literacy, education and career guidance.

  7. DAY7

    My future, my choices

    Mentorship, goal-setting, talent presentations, commitments and graduation.

Activities include small-group discussions, Q&A sessions, sports, team building, drama, poetry, music, storytelling and mentorship. Girls are grouped by age (10–13 and 14–18) so every discussion suits their stage.

Every girl goes home with an empowerment pack

  • A set of reusable sanitary pads, with guidance on use
  • Bathing soap, toothbrush and toothpaste
  • Two pairs of underwear
  • Exercise books, pens and pencils
  • An LMF empowerment booklet and gift bag
  • A certificate of completion

Safe, supervised and accountable

  • Written parent or guardian consent and participant assent for every girl
  • A designated child safeguarding focal person, with confidential reporting and referral pathways
  • 15 vetted female supervisors, including overnight supervision
  • Safe, separate sleeping, sanitation and bathing arrangements
  • First aid on site and emergency medical referral arrangements
  • Pre- and post-programme assessments, attendance records and a final report to donors

Questions

How do I pay after making a pledge?

Pay by bank deposit or transfer using the account details beside the form, or call/WhatsApp us on 0705 834 784 for M-Pesa and other options. Use your name as the payment reference so we can match it to your pledge.

Can I give from outside Kenya?

Yes. Make a pledge with the form and email us at lonahmaikofoundation@gmail.com — we will send international transfer details and confirm receipt.

Will I get a receipt and an update?

Yes. We confirm every gift we receive, and supporters receive a report on what the programme achieved, how many girls took part and how the funds were spent.

What happens if the appeal raises more than the goal?

Any amount above what the programme needs goes to continued mentorship and empowerment activities for the participating girls through schools and communities.

Can my church, school or company sponsor a group?

Absolutely. Choose an amount for the number of girls you want to sponsor (KSh 6,366 per girl) and mention your group in the message box. We will contact you to arrange recognition and reporting.
